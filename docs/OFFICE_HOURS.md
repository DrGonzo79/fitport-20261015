# Office Hours diagnosis

## Customer and desperate moment
The customer is not “accessible travelers.” It is a cruise buyer with a known walking, standing, stair, terrain or stamina constraint—or their family planner—deciding whether one named excursion is safe enough to buy. The desperate moment is before the cancellation window closes, when the cruise line’s adjective is too vague and free agents are incentivized to close a booking.

## Painful status quo
Buyers triangulate a sales page, old forum anecdotes and a phone rep. None describe the approach, mandatory versus optional walking, seating intervals, pace or exit route in one comparable record. The failure cost is physical distress, lost money and being separated abroad.

## Narrowest paid wedge
A 24-hour **single-excursion fit check for $29**: one traveler profile, one option-specific demand map, sources and dates, conflicts, unknowns, three operator questions, and a FIT/RISKY/DO NOT BOOK recommendation. No trip booking and no generic destination guide.

## Observation and validation
For 14 days, answer 20 existing mobility threads without pitching. Invite ten posters to a free concierge check; observe which facts require calls and minutes per report. Then offer five $29 slots. Pass if 3/5 pay, median delivery is under 45 minutes after templates, and at least 80% say the decision changed or became clearer. Kill/reshape if users accept free-agent reassurance, sourcing exceeds 90 minutes, or forum moderators reject the channel.

## Future fit
Repeated checks create an evidence graph keyed by ship, operator, excursion, departure date and demand type. Only after paid repetition should this become a planner, agent tool or data API. The founder advantage is trusted judgment and current source provenance, not AI prose.
