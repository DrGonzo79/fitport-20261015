# CEO review

## Challenge
The premise risks becoming expensive research dressed as SaaS. Buyers can ask free agents; search demand is nearly absent; one incumbent already owns accessibility data and booking distribution. A generic “accessibility score” would be indefensible and dangerous. The report’s $2M–$3M ceiling assumes evidence reuse without proving reports can be produced accurately at margin.

The original broad audience also hides the purchase moment. Distribution depends on communities that punish self-promotion. Architecture can overbuild before proving five payments. AI extraction creates a false-confidence failure mode; conditions change by departure.

## Decisions after review
1. Narrow to one named cruise excursion and explicit functional thresholds; never age/diagnosis scoring.
2. Sell a $29 human-reviewed check before building accounts, payments or scraping infrastructure.
3. Make source class, date, confidence and UNKNOWN first-class; deterministic rules own the verdict.
4. Gate the business on 3/5 paid offers, <45-minute median labor, and decision-impact feedback.
5. Answer communities usefully without links first; test permission-based DMs and referrals rather than assuming forum access.
6. Defer booking and full-trip planning. Build the evidence graph only when repeated paid checks demonstrate reuse.
7. Add discrepancy/override/staleness metrics and mandatory analyst review to the production architecture.

**Verdict:** worth a 14-day concierge test, not yet worth a software company. The prototype tests clarity of the core domain operation, not market demand.
